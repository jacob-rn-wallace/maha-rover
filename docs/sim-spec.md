# Browser simulation of al-maha on the lunar surface

**Goal (owner, 2026-11-08):** nothing can be bought or physically tested for the foreseeable future, so the current goal is a
high-fidelity simulation of the rover on the lunar surface that anyone can run and drive in a browser from GitHub (in spirit
like PUTvision/LunarSim, which is a Unity and ROS 2 Linux binary; this one is a static web page). Fidelity first, then
optimisation, then phones (owner's order).

## Architecture (decided by the assistant, owner deferred the choice)
| Part | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rendering | three.js (WebGL2) | mature, runs on GitHub Pages as a static site, glTF native |
| Physics | Rapier (WASM, `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) | deterministic, fast, revolute and prismatic joints, heightfield and trimesh colliders, runs in a worker later |
| Model | glTF binary exported from the build123d CAD | the CAD stays the single source of truth: `hardware/sim/export_sim.py` |
| Build | Vite + TypeScript, built by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` | repo Settings > Pages > Source "GitHub Actions" is the one manual step |
| Fidelity-critical models | written in TypeScript, unit-checked against the docs | wheel-soil contact, the differential, the motor, power, camera |

## What is in the repo now (phase 1)
- `hardware/sim/export_sim.py` splits the assembly (187 parts, 50.9k triangles) into 9 rigid bodies and writes
  `sim/public/rover.glb` (1.7 MB) and `sim/public/rover.json` (joints, masses, centres of mass, inertia, camera).
  Bodies: chassis; link plate L and R (each with its two motors and a differential side gear); four wheels; mast pan; camera head.
  Body masses come from `hardware/mass/replica.py` (total 9.58 kg, as `docs/replica-mass.md`), the inertia from the meshes at uniform density.
- `sim/` is the web app: it loads the model on a placeholder plane and exercises every joint (pan, tilt, differential links, wheels).
- Axes: glTF Y up, metres; rover X forward stays X, rover Y (left) becomes -Z, rover Z (up) becomes Y.

## Visual layer (not in the CAD checks or the mass budget)
`hardware/sim/export_sim.py` and `hardware/sim/detail.py` add detail to the exported model only, so the CAD, its fit checks and the 9.58 kg estimate stay as they are:
- T-slot profiles (20 x 20, 20 x 40, 40 x 40: slots, diagonal webs, bore) in place of the plain extrusion boxes, with drilled holes kept; typical catalogue slot sizes, ASSUMPTION.
- Real-world materials by part (cast and sheet aluminium, anodised T-slot, black plastic, black cord, glossy dark cells, ASA wheels); the finishes are ASSUMPTIONS.
- Head and base: rounded vertical edges and a lid seam on the pan base box (seam 15 mm below the top, Takachi cover height) with four lid screws; a lid seam 10 mm behind the front face of the camera box (ASSUMPTION, not from the drawing); a PG7 hex gland with a domed cap nut; the lens with a grooved focus ring, front glass and retaining ring; two M5 bolts through the bracket web (positions ASSUMPTION).
- No Rashid-only features are added (owner, 2026-11-08).

## Lunar look (`sim/src/lunar.ts`, first version)
A low sun (14 degrees elevation, a parameter), hard shadows from a 4096 shadow map, a black sky with 4000 seeded stars, a reflection environment of black sky over sunlit regolith (so metals reflect the ground and not a room), and a flat
ground with procedural multi-scale regolith albedo and bump. Look-development values, not measurements: the albedo (about 0.12 to 0.2 effective) and the sun and exposure settings are choices. No craters, rocks or relief yet (phase 3's terrain), no earthshine, no dust.

## Phase 2: driving physics (done 2026-11-08, `sim/src/physics.ts`)
Rapier at 480 Hz fixed steps, lunar gravity 1.62 m/s2, flat ground. Nine rigid bodies from `rover.json`, with the CAD's mass, centre of mass and inertia tensor (mesh-derived, uniform density).
- **Differential:** the two link plates are separate revolute joints on the chassis, coupled by a stiff spring and damper so that angle L + angle R = 0 (k = 600 N.m/rad, c = 6 N.m.s/rad, tuned for critical damping of the sum mode; not gears, so friction and backlash are not modelled).
- **Drive:** each wheel has the Pololu 37D #4746 model from `docs/drive-spec.md`: R = 12 V / 5.5 A = 2.18 ohm, back-EMF constant 0.0111 V.s/rad from 76 rpm and 0.2 A no load, gearbox efficiency 0.75 (the value the datasheet's 0.59 N.m at 0.74 A implies), 7.4 V pack, current cap 1.6 A, shorted-terminal braking at zero duty. The motor rotor's inertia seen through the 131:1 gearbox (0.017 kg.m2, from an ASSUMED 1e-6 kg.m2 rotor) is added to each wheel.
- **Wheels:** collision shapes from the CAD mesh: a rim cylinder and 14 convex grouser hulls (plates thickened from 0.8 to 3 mm to stop tunnelling). Wheels collide with the ground only. Ground friction 0.8 and wheel 0.9 are the flat-plane placeholders (`?flat=1`); on the terrain the soil model below replaces them.
- **Mast:** pan (limit +-160 degrees, the design's 320 degree stop) and tilt (+-90) servos as PD loops, torque capped at the STS3215's 1.91 N.m stall, target slewed at its 52 rpm no-load speed; gains are tuning choices. The mast and head have no collision shapes yet.
- **Chassis collision:** the convex hull of the fixed structure on a 6 mm grid.

Scripted check (`?test=1`, 7.4 V, flat ground, grouser tip radius 115 mm):
| test | result |
|---|---|
| parked 5 s | still: 0.001 m/s, motor currents under 0.03 A |
| full throttle 6 s | steady 0.53 m/s |
| brake from speed | stops in about 1 s, no tipping |
| full turn command | spins in place at 102 deg/s (clockwise for a right turn), about 35% wheel slip |
| mast pan and tilt | reach +160 and +90 degrees |

**What the first runs showed about the design:**
- **Speed:** 0.53 m/s at full duty, above `drive-spec.md`'s 0.49 m/s no-load figure, because that used the 100 mm rim radius and the wheel rolls on its 115 mm grouser tips on hard ground (derived: 47 rpm x 0.115 m x 2 pi = 0.566 m/s before slip). On soft soil the effective radius will be smaller.
- **Braking tips the rover:** it tips nose-over if it decelerates harder than about 0.68 x g = 1.1 m/s2 on the Moon (CAD centre of mass z 235 mm, x +23 mm, half wheelbase 136.5 mm; the threshold scales with g). With zero duty the 37D shorts its terminals and a full brake through the 1.6 A cap is about 5 m/s2; the first test flipped to pitch -100 degrees. The sim limits duty change to 1.5 per second (about 0.8 m/s2). The real motor controller firmware needs the same deceleration limit; this is a finding for the electronics, not only the sim.
- **Wheel spin and friction:** with the placeholder friction the rover can spin in place; with the soil model it turns at about 70 to 100 deg/s (see Soil model).

## Soil model (done 2026-11-09; `sim/src/soil.ts`, `sim/src/physics.ts`, `sim/tests/soil-check.ts`)
**Sources.** (1) Hurrell, Takehana, Tanaka, Uno, Busoud and Yoshida, "Traction Performance Evaluation for a Rashid-1 Rover Wheel", Space Science Reviews 221:37 (2025), doi 10.1007/s11214-025-01164-8 (private library S4): single-wheel tests and DEM of the Rashid-1 wheel (rim radius 100 mm, width 80 mm, 14 grousers of 20 mm, 1.18 kg) in FJS-1 lunar simulant at 25% relative density (bulk density 1740 kg/m3, angle of repose 38 degrees, cohesion 2.4 kN/m2) at the rover's load of 24.5 N per wheel on Earth, and 4.1 N in lunar gravity by DEM.
Its Table 4 (lunar columns) is the target; its finding is that the traction coefficient (net pull over load) is the same in lunar gravity as on Earth at the same mass while the forces scale by 1/6, so the model is per unit load. (2) Tuned parameters of the MBRSC regolith in the Advance Simulation paper (Reece pressure-sinkage model with a grouser term and the Wong shear model, Vortex Studio; private library S22): friction angle 23 degrees, cohesion 0, shear modulus K 0.036 m, sinkage exponent 1.1, weight density 15620 N/m3, kc' 0.63, kphi' 80. **Not used**: its tuned parameters fit a different simulant, its quoted test-wheel size as printed (diameter 0.1 m, width 7.5 mm, 12 grousers of 10 mm) disagrees with the Hurrell paper's wheel and with the 60 N loads in its Table III, and its untuned parameters were 40% off its own bench drawbar pull (6.2 N against 10.2 N; within about 10% after tuning, Table III). They are recorded here as the softer alternative (tan 23 degrees = 0.42, against 0.86 for FJS-1).
**Table 4, lunar, FJS-1 (slip s = (w R_rim - v) / (w R_rim)):** at 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50% slip: net pull / load 0.28, 0.34, 0.38, 0.42, 0.45, 0.49; wheel torque 0.22, 0.26, 0.29, 0.31, 0.32, 0.35 N.m (load 4.07 N); total sinkage 5.1, 7.8, 11.2, 15.6, 20.5, 26.5 mm. Its Fig. 5 shows the experiment 10 to 15% below the DEM at 30 to 50% slip; the DEM is used as it is the only lunar-gravity result.
**Derived here (ASSUMPTION where marked):** gross thrust is the torque over R (0.54 to 0.86 of the load) and the motion resistance is gross thrust minus net pull (0.26 to 0.37 of the load). The paper's slip is referred to the rim, but the grousers set the kinematic radius, so thrust at "0% slip" is not zero; referring slip to the grouser tips, s_e = 1 - (1 - s) / 1.2, thrust is zero at s_e = 0 and a least-squares fit gives a Janosi-Hanamoto curve T / load = 0.86 (1 - exp(-s_e / 0.18)) and a resistance of (0.237 + 0.229 s_e) load; the net pull is within 0.04 of the load at all six points. tan(38 degrees) = 0.78 against 0.86: the cohesion makes up the difference. The flight wheel's grousers are 15 mm (tip radius 115 mm), the test wheel's 20 mm: the same curve is used with the sim wheel's own tip radius (**ASSUMPTION**: the shear curve does not depend on the grouser length).
**In the sim:** Coulomb friction is off at the wheels (friction 0, combine rule minimum); each substep the contact normal load of each wheel is read from the Rapier contacts (smoothed over 30 ms), the tip-referred slip from the wheel's rim speed and hub velocity, and the soil force applied: thrust and side force at the contact point as one combined-slip vector of magnitude N T(s), resistance at the hub opposing its motion. The reaction torque on the wheel comes out of the thrust acting at the contact point, so the motor sees the load. Sinkage is not simulated (rigid ground, the wheel rides on its rim; the grouser hulls do not collide with the ground and are kept for rocks); the paper's sinkage for the current slip is shown in the HUD. Rapier's reported contact impulses sum to (n + 1) / n times the weight for n = 8 solver iterations (measured), so loads are scaled by n / (n + 1).
**Checks (`npm run check:soil`, 28 s simulated in about 5 s of Node time):** settled wheel loads 4.1, 4.2, 3.6, 3.7 N (sum 15.6 N, weight 15.5 N); full throttle on flat ground 0.515 m/s at 7% slip (Coulomb: 0.54); coasting and braking stop it; turn in place 65 to 100 deg/s (Coulomb: 190); a 12 s drive up a ramp of 5, 10, 15, 20 degrees: 0.49, 0.45, 0.42, 0.36 m/s at slip 10, 15, 22, 34%; at 25 degrees the rover tips over backwards (the static tip angle is 26 degrees, `docs/mass-budget.md`). On the real terrain `?test=1&terrain=1`: 0.52 m/s, turn -71 deg/s, brakes to rest. The start-up transient seen with rigid grousers (pitch 6 degrees in the first second) is gone.
**Not modelled:** sinkage and its effect on the chassis ground clearance, wheel tracks, slip sinkage, lateral soil flow, bulldozing against rocks, dust; gravity scaling beyond the paper's finding; the difference between FJS-1 and real lunar regolith at the landing site.

## Rocks (done 2026-11-09; `sim/src/rocks.ts`, `npm run check:rocks`, `npm run check:soil -- rocks|field`)
**Sources.** (1) Joulaud et al., Space Science Reviews 220:65 (2024): 7232 boulders mapped by hand on LROC NAC images in the 8 km ellipse FR-11 in Atlas crater, **144 per km2**. Objects under about 1.5 to 2 m cannot be detected at 0.5 to 1 m/px, so the count is of boulders of about 2 m and more; the fields lie near the ellipse centre (south-west, by a large degraded crater), in the north-east and on topographic highs, the plains elsewhere being poor. The paper gives no size distribution (checked in full); the mapped positions are only in its figures. (2) Bandfield et al., JGR Planets 116, E00H02 (2011), after Cintala and McBride (1995): from 769 blocks (2 to 11 m and more) counted on LROC images of the central peak of King Crater, the cumulative areal fraction of rocks of diameter d or more is **c = 0.082 d^-1.326** (d in metres); their counts under 3 m are probably incomplete. The number of rocks of d or more per m2 follows as N = A d^-3.326, A = 4 K b / (pi (b + 2)), c = K d^-b.
**Model.** The law is scaled so that N(2 m) = 144 per km2: K = 0.00285 (the areal fraction of rocks of 1 m and more, 0.285%). Rocks of 0.12 to 14 m are generated deterministically per grid cell (seeded by the cell, a Poisson count, diameters by inverse transform of the truncated power law), in three tiers drawn as instanced meshes: stones 0.12 to 0.5 m within 50 m (about 3700 on screen), rocks 0.5 to 2 m within 300 m (2000), boulders 2 to 14 m within 1.5 km (1000); rebuilt in 5 to 20 ms when the rover has moved 16, 64 or 256 m. Shapes are lumpy ellipsoids (vertical axis 0.35 to 0.7 of the width), buried by 20 to 40% of their height, grey 0.75 to 1.25 of an albedo of about 0.17, lit by the terrain shadow textures. The spawn point is kept clear.
Physics: convex hulls of the rocks of 0.2 m and more (the smaller stones are drawn only): stones within 8 m, rocks within 30 m, boulders within 60 m of the rover (about 100 to 400 colliders), friction 0.6 with the maximum combine rule (so the wheels, friction 0 under the soil model, grip them), colliding with the chassis, wheels and grousers; the soil model sees only the ground's contacts. `?rocks=0` leaves them out. **T** stands the rover upright where it is, keeping its heading.
**ASSUMPTIONS (not in the sources):** (1) King Crater's exponent holds on the Atlas floor and down to 0.12 m, a factor of 17 below the smallest mapped boulders (the counted areal fraction of rocks of 12 cm and more is then about 2.5 to 4.7%, probably more than the real plains carry: a Golombek and Rapp style law that saturates at small sizes would give fewer); (2) the 144 per km2 is complete at 2 m (complete at 3 m would give K = 0.011, four times the rocks); (3) the density is a smooth random field (log-normal, fields a few hundred metres across) times a slope factor (1 + (slope / 8 degrees)^2, at most 5), normalised to the tile mean: boulder fields exist, but their mapped places are not used; (4) shapes and burial. Over the whole 23 km2 of the generator's area the count of rocks of 2 m and more is 145 per km2 (target 144); the smaller sizes, counted over smaller areas, scatter with the density field.
**Diviner rock abundance (the units question of the hand-off):** the cumulative law with K = 0.00285 gives 0.285% of the surface covered by rocks of 1 m and more; the Diviner map reports the areal fraction of rocks (Bandfield: mostly 0.002 to 0.005 on the Moon, so reported as fractions or percent of 1). Flahaut et al. 2024 give 0.0029 for FR-11, which agrees if it is a fraction (0.29%; the column's "(%)" is a mislabel like that of its slope column, a fraction by its footnote). Joulaud et al. give 0.029%, ten times less: probably a slip. Unresolved in the papers themselves; the agreement above is a plausibility check, not a proof (K rests on assumption 2).
**Results (the rover on flat ground, blind at full throttle, `npm run check:soil -- field|rocks`):** a 6 cm high rock is crossed with a pitch excursion of 35 degrees; a rock as high as the wheel radius (10 cm) stops the rover and tips it onto its nose, as does a 20 cm rock. This follows from the drive: a 1.6 A motor stalls at about 13 N at the wheel, and four of them against an immovable obstacle (53 N) exceed what the 15.5 N rover can take without tipping; a design consequence: the motor controller needs stall or obstacle detection, not only the current cap. In the generated field, from four starts, the rover drove 53 m, was stuck at 9 m, and tipped twice within 16 m (denser patches): the field is drivable only by steering round the larger stones.
**Not modelled:** rock shadows beyond the 4 m shadow map round the rover (rocks only receive the terrain shadows), rock albedo and shape variety (one lumpy mesh), rocks that roll, regolith dust, clustering round craters, and the rocks beyond the tile (the ring has none).

## Terrain data (decided 2026-11-08)
Real terrain for the high-detail area: a 5 x 5 km window of the LROC Atlas crater DTM (2 m posts, `hardware/sim/terrain/atlas_tile.py`, tile in `sim/public/terrain/`). Beyond it, a low-detail ring (LOLA, 237 m posts); the physics runs only on a patch of about 30 m around the rover
built from the tile plus procedural detail (owner's layering, 2026-11-08).
- **Site, and the criteria behind it:** the DTM is three strips (stereo swaths) with a large empty north-west part (about 43% of the file has no data), so the paper's prime ellipse is not covered and the Hakuto-R impact point (47.581 N, 44.094 E) is outside the raster.
  `hardware/sim/terrain/site_select.py` scores all 828 fully covered 5 x 5 km windows with the method and numbers of the Rashid-1 site-selection paper (Flahaut et al., Space Science Reviews 220:53, 2024, in the private paper library): slopes on 8 m cells (their source is the 7.4 m/px Kaguya TC mosaic),
  at least 95% of slopes under 15 degrees (their verification step), mean slope at most 7.3 degrees (the highest of their Atlas candidate ellipses, whose mean slopes are 6.8 to 7.3 with standard deviation 4.2 to 4.6 and 94 to 96% of pixels under 15 degrees), nearest to their prime ellipse FR-11 (47.53 N, 44.33 E; 6.8 degrees, 96%).
  219 windows pass. The nearest is centred on **47.5676 N, 44.8102 E**, 10.0 km from FR-11: mean slope 6.2 degrees, 97.9% under 15, 30% under 4, relief 305 m (the earlier criteria, which I invented, rejected this for its relief). The tile has a thin vertical stripe near its east edge, probably a seam between data strips.
  The paper's ellipse is an 8 km circle, the sandbox a 5 km square, so the statistics are close but not the same. The windows' slopes come from stereo-matched 2 m posts averaged to 8 m, a different source from the paper's, so they are comparable, not identical.
- **What the paper also gives for the procedural detail (Atlas ellipse FR-11, from NAC mapping):** 7232 mapped boulders in the 8 km ellipse, 144 per km2; Diviner rock abundance low (0.029% in the boulder paper, 0.0029 in the site paper's table: the units differ or one is a typo, to be checked); mean Mini-RF CPR 0.56;
  median regolith thickness 1.2 m (quartiles 1 and 1.7, range 0.5 to 20.2 m) from 293 mapped small craters, so a thin regolith; the ellipse is "generally devoid of fresh impact craters, and dominated by gently rolling plains", with boulder fields near its edges and near large degraded craters (Joulaud et al., Space Science Reviews 220:65, 2024, doi 10.1007/s11214-024-01101-1).
  Boulder size distribution and minimum mapped size were not found in the pages read: needed for the rock model.
- **Viewshed and ring (done 2026-11-09, `hardware/sim/terrain/viewshed.py`, outputs `sim/public/terrain/ring.png`, `ring_mask.png`, `ring.json`, preview `docs/images/viewshed-ring-preview.png`):** a combined elevation model (Atlas 2 m posts block-averaged to 8 m where covered, LDEM_128 elsewhere; on the Atlas area the two differ by a mean of -1.3 m, median -0.3 m, standard deviation 17 m, so no datum shift is applied) is swept from 33 observers (20 on the tile edge, a 3 x 3 lattice at 1.5 km inside) with the camera 0.7 m above ground and the lunar curvature (R 1737.4 km) in the elevation-angle test, 0.05 degree ray spacing, 8 m steps to 16 km and 48 m beyond, to 100 km.
  The ring is a polar grid about the tile centre (1440 azimuth bins, 848 range bins growing by 0.44% per bin from 2.5 km, so cells stay square: 11 m at the tile edge, about 220 m at 50 km, matching the LDEM's 237 m). Each cell stores its height and the fraction of observers that see it.
  Result: 30.7% of the 1.22 million ring cells (375,108) are seen from at least one observer, out to 74 km (so the 100 km limit does not clip it); what is seen is the crater's inner walls and rim, a broken band, and the flat floor between is hidden. Per observer the visible cells range from 30,000 to 196,000.
  Limits: eye height only (a free camera above 0.7 m would see more); the ring beyond the tile edge is 8 m data where the sim's tile is 2 m; where the Atlas ends and the LDEM takes over the model is joined without a step (fixed 2026-11-09, see Seams below); the 0.25 degree azimuth bins are about 320 m wide at 74 km.
- **In the sim (done 2026-11-09; `sim/src/terrain.ts`, `terrainView.ts`, `physics.ts`):** the world frame is x east, y up, z south, origin at the tile centre at the tile's centre height, and the heights carry the lunar curvature (y = h - d^2 / 2R inside the tile, the exact sphere in the ring). The tile is smoothed with a Gaussian of 2 posts (4 m; the product's precision is 2.89 m) and sampled bicubically; below that scale, five octaves of deterministic procedural noise (8, 4, 2, 1, 0.5 m wavelength, amplitude 0.006 x wavelength, each octave's lattice turned by its own angle) are added: **ASSUMPTION**, a look-development roughness of a few millimetres to a few centimetres, to be replaced from the soil and rock data.
  The rover spawns on the flattest spot within 400 m of the centre (worst slope to eight neighbours 6 m away; now x -200 m, z +160 m, i.e. 160 m south-west). Visuals: a geometry clipmap of four levels (0.25 m cells over 64 m, 1 m over 256 m, 4 m over 1 km, a fixed 20 m level over the tile), world-aligned lattices rebuilt in steps of 4, 16 and 80 m, holes where the finer level lies and the finer level's edge vertices stitched to the coarser level's linear interpolation (no cracks); the ring mesh beyond the tile uses only the cells some observer sees, plus a one-cell margin (796,000 triangles). Logarithmic depth buffer, camera far plane 200 km, stars drawn as a background layer.
  Physics: a Rapier heightfield collider of 32 m at 0.25 m cells, rebuilt (snapped to 4 m) when the rover is 4 m from its centre, with `FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES` (without it a rover on even a perfectly flat heightfield wheelies and stalls); four hard walls 20 m inside the tile edge, and the HUD warns within 100 m of them (owner's "soft boundary" is only the warning for now).
  Checked 2026-11-09 (headless Chrome, Coulomb friction): `?test=1&terrain=1` drove at 0.54 m/s, turned at -93 deg/s, braked to rest, mast moved; 20 m driven through five collider re-centrings with steady speed. A start-up transient (pitch up to 6 degrees) was traced to the rigid grousers hopping on the heightfield and is gone with the soil model (below).
  URL switches: `?flat=1` the old flat plane (`?test=1` implies it unless `?terrain=1`), `?soil=0` Coulomb friction on the terrain (`?soil=1` the soil model on the flat plane), `?detail=0` no procedural roughness, `?smooth=posts` the smoothing sigma. `?cam=` is now relative to the spawn point.
  Seams and shadows (done 2026-11-09): (1) The ring showed a lit line and a block pattern west of the tile. The line was a step in the elevation model where the Atlas ends (the Atlas and LDEM differ by 17 m, standard deviation): `viewshed.py` now shifts the LDEM by the Atlas-minus-LDEM difference averaged over 200 m round the Atlas edge, fading out over 1.5 km (and smoothly where Atlas cells thin out: a hard cut at the edge of the averaging made a one-cell spike, the line that remained), and eases the Atlas's outer 150 m onto the same surface. The blocks were the LDEM's 237 m pixels sampled bilinearly: now a cubic spline. A faint band where the surface detail changes from 8 m to 237 m data remains. (2) The tile mesh's fixed level is now 10 m (was 20 m), the ring sinks by up to 2 m under the tile, and the ring is always drawn within 150 m of the tile: the ring had poked through the sagging tile mesh at the edge. Ring cells no observer can see are not drawn at all: from a camera far above the rover the ground beyond the tile shows holes (by design: the rover's eye is at 0.7 m).
  Terrain shadows: `hardware/sim/terrain/shadow.py` sweeps the sun direction over the elevation model (Atlas tile, then the 8 m and 48 m grids) and writes two lit-fraction textures (`shadow_tile.png`: the tile at 4 m, `shadow_coarse.png`: 100 km round at 100 m); `terrainShadow.ts` multiplies them into the sun's light on the terrain and on the rover's materials. They hold the fixed sun (14 degrees elevation, azimuth 40); if `lunar.ts` uses another, they are not applied. 0.4% of the tile and 3.5% of the 200 km square are in shadow (the tile is gentle ground under a 14 degree sun: shadows lie in the crater rim's lee, along the tile's north-east edge, and in hollows). Soft edge 2 m on the shadow side (a fixed simplification of the 0.53 degree sun disc); lunar curvature not in the shadow geometry; blockers beyond 100 km not modelled. The renderer's own shadow map still covers the 4 m round the rover.
  Not done: no craters or rocks, the regolith bump texture shows water-like ripples at grazing angles and a regular weave from above (already so on the flat plane: needs macro variation and a distance fade). `?hide=0,1,2,3,4` hides a terrain level (3 is the tile) or the ring (4): for finding seams.
- **Accuracy:** the product states 2.89 m vertical precision and 3.47 to 13.0 m RMS against LOLA tracks, so small features come from procedural detail, not from the data.

## Fidelity criteria (proposed; the owner has not confirmed them)
1. **Geometry:** the sim rover is the CAD, to the millimetre; wheels have their 14 grousers as real collision geometry.
2. **Mass:** per-body mass and centre of mass from `replica.py`; lunar gravity 1.62 m/s2.
3. **Mechanism:** the passive differential is simulated as the mechanism it is (links coupled, angle L = -angle R), not a rocker; skid steering emerges from four driven wheels.
4. **Drive:** each wheel driven by the Pololu 37D motor model (torque-speed line, 1.6 A current cap, gear ratio 131:1) from `docs/drive-spec.md`; the battery sag and power from `docs/power-spec.md`.
5. **Terrain:** procedural lunar surface (crater size-frequency distribution, rocks, regolith relief), real lunar lighting (sun at a low elevation, black sky, hard shadows, earthshine optional), regolith appearance; wheel-soil interaction through a simple Bekker-type sinkage and slip model, tuned to published lunar regolith values (cited when added).
6. **Mast and camera:** pan and tilt servos with speed and range limits; the HQ camera view (field of view, resolution, no auto-exposure tricks) shown as a picture-in-picture.
7. **Telemetry:** speed, wheel slip, link-plate angle, tilt, power draw, battery state, solar input.
8. **Validation:** static tip angles and the 20 degree slope climb from `replica.py` and `docs/suspension-spec.md` must reproduce in the sim.

## Surface realism: what other lunar simulators do (2026-11-09; web search, abstracts and the pages that opened, not full papers)
Sources: OmniLRS (arXiv 2309.08997; Isaac Sim, path tracing; its repository is named LunarSim too, but the paper called LunarSim is the Poznan one below); the Chrono::Sensor lunar regolith model (arXiv 2410.04371); Menon, Walker, Szafir, Fong and Burns, 'Efficient Photometric Models of the Lunar Regolith for Virtual Reality Simulation' (a 2019 one-page poster, CU Boulder and NASA Ames, in the private repo's `reference-material/`, read), LunarSim by Pieczynski, Ptak, Kraft and Drapikowski (Applied Sciences 13(22), 12401, 2023, doi 10.3390/app132212401, private repo `papers/`, read); the SurRender and Lunar-G2R papers (snippets); NASA's Gazebo rover simulator (NTRS 20190027571, snippet).
- **No tiled texture.** OmniLRS builds geometry: 1 m per post DEMs, upsampled by GAN and then crater-decorated to about 4 cm per pixel (2.5 cm in its large-scale mode); craters of 0.5 to 10 m from 100 half-profiles taken from SLDEM, normalised and fitted with cubic splines, placed with hard-core Poisson processes in three density classes; rocks from photogrammetry and Apollo scans with the detail baked into normal maps, placed by Poisson and Thomas point processes. It states that no public lunar material exists and that its gravel textures from Polyhaven are not lunar. NASA's Gazebo simulator does the same (fractal upsampling plus craters and rocks from size-frequency laws). Chrono::Sensor uses no normal map at all: the micro-relief is real geometry (one voxel per particle).
- **The regolith is not Lambertian.** The standard model is Hapke's; real-time versions use Lommel-Seeliger single scattering plus a shadow-hiding opposition surge (Chrono's Table I: single-scattering albedo 0.03257, B0 = 1.80238, h = 0.07145, roughness 23.4 degrees, filling factor 0.3; PANGU-derived values quoted by another paper: albedo 0.33, h 0.05, B0 0.95). The consequences visible in photographs: no darkening towards the limb, a bright flat look when the sun is behind the camera, and a washed-out texture looking along the sun. Lunar-G2R (2026) argues that a spatially uniform BRDF is itself a limit and predicts per-point BRDF parameters from a DEM.
- **Path tracing** (OmniLRS) gives the pitch-black shadows and hard contrast; the cameras add sensor noise, vignetting and saturation (Chrono: Poisson-Gaussian noise, cos^4 vignetting, a 16 bit HDR response); OmniLRS lists lens flare, noise and saturation as sim-to-real gaps it does not model.
Applied here (2026-11-09, `sim/src/regolith.ts`, `terrain.ts`): (1) the tiled texture is gone; the albedo mottling (metre to 90 m patches, finer grain) and the centimetre-scale normal relief are world-space noise evaluated per pixel, octaves fading under two pixel footprints; (2) the sun's light is weighted Lommel-Seeliger with Chrono's opposition surge (normalised to 1 at a phase angle of 30 degrees, weight capped at 3), on the ground and on the rocks; (3) small craters from 0.75 to 48 m (six tiers) in `Terrain.craters`, by an equilibrium size law (cumulative N = 3% x 1.54 D^-2 per m2: the 1.54 saturation law is from memory, not read, and 3% is a look choice), bowl and rim profile, depth/diameter 0.04 to 0.20, deterministic per cell, shared by mesh, collider and rock placement. Not done: half-crater profiles from real data, photogrammetry rocks, camera noise and vignetting on the mast view, per-point albedo from the DEM, the Hapke roughness term. The Chrono parameters are fitted for 30 S to 30 N and this site is at 47.6 N.

Read later (2026-11-09): **LunarSim (Pieczynski et al. 2023)** is a Unity scene of 950 x 950 m with hand-made craters, hand-placed rock models, blended albedo and normal textures and screen-space post effects (colour grading, ambient occlusion, contact shadows, reflections); no photometric model, and it names more realistic textures as future work: nothing there beyond what is applied above. **The Colorado poster** confirms the approach: sun as one directional light (a slightly reddish 255, 235, 238), the base terrain from LROC NAC elevation models fractally expanded from about 0.5 m to sub-millimetre, two BRDFs (full Hapke with five parameters for Mare Imbrium from Sato et al. 2014, JGR Planets 119:1775, and the cheaper Hapke-Lommel-Seeliger with parameters from Jensen et al. 2001's night-sky model), no ray tracing because the single-scattering albedo is about 0.15; its future work lists energy-conserving normalisation (the opposition weight here is normalised by hand to 1 at 30 degrees: not energy-conserving either), normal and height maps from observed fractal parameters, and craters and boulders from size-frequency distributions (done here for craters). **Lead not followed:** Sato et al. 2014 maps the Hapke parameters over the Moon, which could replace Chrono's 30 S to 30 N values with site values for 47.6 N.
