# Mast specification (replica)

Replica mast, head and camera, **fixed upright**. Dimensions are from the original's CAD figures
(`original-dimensions.md` 3j, derived) where they exist; the rest is the replica's own design and
ASSUMPTION. CAD and checks: `hardware/mast/mast.py`.

**Stowing and deploying are deferred** (decision 2026-11-07). The original folds the mast flat under
120 mm and springs it up on landing [S19]; the replica's mast stands fixed for now. The earlier hinged design
(pedestal, hinge pin, fold sweep) is in commit `20aec90`, and the reasons a spring hinge does not fit are in
`electronics-spec.md` (a bus-servo hinge is the likely route when stowing is wanted).

## Geometry

Revised 2026-11-07 (owner's request): **the mast is a T-slot aluminium extrusion itself.** A 40 x 40 mm extrusion stands on a
spindle flange (the whole mast turns on a pan drive in a housing on the deck, below) and carries the bent sheet bracket and head (section "Built head" below) on its top end.

**Pan base (added 2026-11-07, owner's request: the original's pan motor is in the block at the mast base; redone 2026-11-08 around a die-cast box).** `hardware/mast/base.py`: a **Takachi AL080807** IP65 die-cast aluminium box (80 x 75 x 70 mm, 347 g, gasketed, M4
cover screws; drawing AL080807 (2025): cavity 70.5 x 66.8 at the mouth, interior height 62.75, base 55 deep, cover 15 deep; stated) stands on the base plate (3 mm since the mass second pass of 2026-11-08) with its **lid on top** (z 351 to 421). The original's base block measures about 79 mm wide and 73 mm tall with its
top near z 425 (measured on Fig. 1, `original-dimensions.md` 3m): the box matches it. Inside: the pan servo (STS3215, shaft vertical on the mast axis, long side along y so the 45.2 mm body clears the 70.5 mm cavity by 0.1 mm; z 354.6 to 391.8), a coupling disc
on its upper horn, and an aluminium bearing tower (OD 52) hung under the lid holding two 30 x 42 x 7 mm bearings (6806 size) 12.6 mm apart, the upper one against the lid's underside (z 401.3 and 413.9). The aluminium spindle (tube 30/22) runs up through them and
a 36 mm hole in the lid to a 60 mm flange at z 423 to 429 on which the extrusion stands (now z 429 to 597, 168 mm, 0.31 kg). No cable passes through the box or the spindle: the harness hangs outside the mast in a loose loop, as the original's does (below). The servo only turns the mast; the bearings carry the weight and bending, with the lid carrying the bearings: mast, bearings, tower, lid, four M4 cover screws, base,
box, plate. With the bearings only 12.6 mm apart the radial load at 1 g sideways is 156 N each (468 N at 3 g) from the 1.96 N.m moment, three times the earlier 30 mm spacing; far below a 6806 bearing's rating (not checked). The cables run down the extrusion slots, through the hollow
spindle bore is empty. Not designed: the lid's stiffness under that load (a 3.6 mm cast cap; a plate under it may be needed), the gasket and a seal where the spindle leaves the lid, fixing the box to the base plate (the box has two M4
external mounting holes) and the plate to the deck, the compliant joint between servo horn and disc, axial retention of the bearings, the hard stop for the 320 degree range, the cable loop. Mass: the box is 0.347 kg against 0.145 kg for the printed housing it replaces.

**Departure from the original.** The original's mast tube measures about 58 mm square [3j]. The replica's extrusion is 40 mm, so the
visible column is slimmer below the 58 mm printed neck. A 60 x 60 extrusion would match, but at about 4.17 kg/m it would weigh 0.93 kg
(0.52 kg more) and the mass margin would shrink to about 0.1 kg; a 40 x 60 would be a compromise (not looked up). The extrusion's end is
tapped (M8) to take the neck's base plate; its slots carry the camera and servo cables, and the centre bore can pass a connector lead.

The original's mast is additively manufactured ULTEM 1010 with titanium and aluminium parts [S19]; the replica's neck, arm and head are printed
(material not chosen).

## Actuation and the head's parts (2026-11-07)

(Superseded by the built head below.) The head was no longer solid boxes: `hardware/mast/head.py` builds the neck, C-arm, camera housing and camera tube as **2 mm printed shells** (ASSUMPTION: PETG, solid
walls) with real parts inside. The outer sizes still follow the CAD figures (3j).

- **Pan servo (vertical axis, now in the base, see above; its description here applies):** a Feetech STS3215-class servo modelled from the Waveshare ST3215 2D drawing (2022-06-08) and its STEP model: body
  45.22 x 24.72 x 32 mm, output shaft 10.11 mm from one end of the long side, a 19.2 mm horn disc at each end, 37.25 mm overall along the shaft. The Waveshare part is the same
  size as the 45.2 x 24.7 x 35 mm STS3215 of the retailers and the Feetech datasheet; the equivalence is assumed, and the Feetech datasheet read has no drawing. The shaft is on
  the mast axis and the long side points +y. It stands on the housing floor (the earlier neck position is replaced). The horn hole pattern (a 14 mm pattern on the drawing) and the
  mounting ears are not modelled, and the compliant coupling between the horn and the coupling disc is not designed.
- **Tilt servo (horizontal axis, in the C-arm):** the same servo, shaft along y at z 660, body below the shaft, centred in the arm (y 28.9 to 66.1 in a 27 to 68 cavity).
- **Camera:** a Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 (standard lens) modelled from the product brief p. 4 (PCB 25 x 23.862 x 1.12, four 2.2 mm holes, 10.8 mm square lens block, 11.3 mm
  total, lens centre 12.5 mm from the side and 14.4 mm from the bottom edge) inside the printed 35 x 35 x 73 mm CAM-1 tube [S12], its lens against a 12 mm aperture in the front wall.
  The FPC connector block is ASSUMPTION. The standard lens (75 degrees) is closer to the original's 80 degrees [S12] than the wide one (120).
- **Wires:** from the head down the extrusion's slots; the cable path, strain relief and the connector at the lid are not designed.

## CAD checks

- Top at 700 (stated); camera axis 660; lens front 214.4 (front axle at 136.5 + 77.9), which is 24 mm ahead of the body's front end (190).
- The base plate lies wholly on the deck plate (5776 of 5776 mm2) and does not touch the body or the cells.
- **Fit:** both servos and the camera module lie wholly inside their shells; no part of the head clashes with another.
- **Pan sweep, plus or minus 180 degrees:** the whole turning mast (spindle, extrusion, neck, arm, head) clears the base plate, housing, servo and bearings (0 mm3 at every 30 degree step).
- **(Superseded by the built head below, which tilts 90 degrees.) Tilt sweep of the printed-shell head: it fails.** With the head as designed, the camera tube touches the housing after **5 degrees** of tilt in either direction, and the whole head tilting as one unit touches
  the neck after 5 degrees too. The original tilts plus or minus 75 degrees [S19]. The cause is the stated geometry: the head occupies z 621 to 700 about the camera axis (z 660), so
  its lower corners sweep down to about z 610 against the neck top at 621, over the part of the neck (y -29 to 8) that lies under the head. **This needs a design decision;** the options
  are in the Open list below.

## Loads

With catalogue and CAD masses (extrusion 0.31 kg and base plate 0.05 kg; die-cast pan box 0.347 kg; spindle, flange, disc and bearing tower 0.12 kg; bearings 0.05 kg; bracket 0.069 kg; die-cast camera box 0.162 kg, printed hat and lens parts and the disk (0.015 kg, ASSUMPTION); camera body stand-in 0.010 kg (ASSUMPTION); two servos 0.11 kg; hardware 0.05 kg;
total 1.37 kg) the centre of mass is 146 mm above the deck. A sideways acceleration of 1 g (a tipping rover) puts about **1.96 N.m** on the base joint; 0.46 N.m on a 20 degree
slope. The extrusion is a stiff column (40 x 40 section); the joint at the plate is the weak point and no stress check has been done.

## Open

- **(Superseded, see the built head below.) Tilt range.** The printed-shell head cannot tilt plus or minus 75 degrees as modelled. Options (not chosen): (a) tilt only a small barrel or the lens inside a housing with a large front opening, which needs a different mechanism from a servo on the tube; (b) cut the tilt range to what fits (about 5 degrees now; a few tens of degrees if the housing's lower corners are rounded off and the neck narrowed under it, not worked out); (c) move the neck's footprint so it does not lie under the housing (the pan axis then moves off the mast centre line); (d) accept a tilt range far below the original's and note it. The original's actual head mechanism is not known from the sources read.

- Servos and camera module are candidates only (`electronics-spec.md`), none bought.
- The wires from the head down the extrusion's slots and into the body: where they enter the deck is reserved in
  `interior-spec.md` (a 15 x 40 x 30 mm zone under the base), but the entry is not designed.
- The plate is held by 4 M4 bolts through the deck into two frame rails (`frame-spec.md`); the bolts themselves, how the plate fixes to the
  extrusion (corner brackets or end-tapped screws) and the joint's strength are not modelled.
- The pan servo is in the base; the tilt servo sits in the arm; the extrusion has no cavity for them.
- Stowing: deferred. The stowed height limit (120 mm above the deck [S19]) is not met by this mast.


## Built head (2026-11-08): bent sheet bracket, `hardware/mast/headb.py`

**This supersedes the printed-shell head described above** (`head.py`: neck, C-arm, camera housing, camera tube, and the 5 degree tilt limit; `head.py` is kept for its servo and camera models). The head is now in `mast.py`
and the assembly render; `hardware/mast/head_concept.py` renders it alone to `docs/images/head-concept.png`. History: v3 (printed arm on an adapter plate, commit `2abb639`), then the owner's sheet-metal sketches
(2026-11-08) gave the bracket. Owner's rule (2026-11-07): keep what gives the original's silhouette and costs nothing, otherwise design for our parts.

- **Bracket (owner's simplified redesign, 2026-11-08, from `mast_simplified_in_situ.step`; outline traced by the owner from the original's front view [S18] Fig. 1):** a 2 mm aluminium **web**, 40 mm wide, z 545 to 690, lying flat on the extrusion's
  +y face (y 20 to 22) with three Ø6.8 holes (z 555, 569 and 583; the first two are the owner's, the third was added with the taller extrusion) for M6 into the centre slot of that face, and a 2 mm **flange** along each side edge (x +-18 to +-20 from the mast axis) standing out to y 54: a point at the extrusion at z 545, a slant to full depth by
  z 566, vertical to z 635, then a slant in to y 36 at the top (z 690). It reaches 93 mm above the extrusion top (597) and carries the head; nothing straddles the extrusion. The tilt servo (STS3215, y 19.5 to 56.7) sits between the flanges against the web, its
  horn through a Ø19.2 hole at z 660, to the disk. Mass 0.070 kg. **The extrusion was raised from 577 to 597** (2026-11-08, on the owner's suggestion) to give the web 52 mm of bolted contact instead of 32: swept by the tilting camera box, contact with the extrusion starts at 65 degrees of tilt with the top at 605, so 597 keeps a margin (clear to 90 degrees both ways, checked in `mast.py`). The original's tube tops out at 577 [3j]; the extra 20 mm is hidden behind the bracket's web from the side but shows in the front view above the original's neck line. **Overlaid on the original's front view the outline follows the original's arm (outer edge, diagonal shoulder, top)**; the flat blank is simply a plate with two edge flanges, so it
  is one bent part (two bends along the long edges, no bends across the flanges). The earlier attempts (flanges running round the bends; mitred flanges; S-shaped plates) were my misreadings or an unmakeable blank and are replaced. Camera lateral
  position: in this version y -30.5 (the sourced figure is 28 right of the centreline [S8]); the disk is 20 mm long (y -0.5 to 19.5).
- **Camera unit** (tilts as one; positions measured against the original's Fig. 1, `original-dimensions.md` 3m): a hollow printed disk (below), a **die-cast aluminium box, Takachi AL060605** (IP65, 64 x 58 x 46 mm, 162 g, gasketed, grey, M4 cover screws on 52 x 46 mm; drawing AL060605 (2025): walls 3.7,
  cavity 56.6 x 50.6, cover cap 9 deep; stated) with the cover at the front (as the cover screws are on the front of the original's camera box), 64 mm wide across y, 58 mm tall, 46 deep, centred on the tilt axis; **the Raspberry Pi HQ camera with a 4 mm CS lens** (below) in it,
  a printed slotted baffle ring and a black hood with a square window on the front, and a flat round hat (an aluminium ground-plane disc and a GNSS patch, Ø70 x 1.5 mm and 35 x 35 x 4 mm, to z 701.5) on top. The lens stands 31 mm out of the cover. **How cubic:** 64 x 58 x 46 (smallest over largest 0.72); the earlier TD6-6-4 was 60 x 60 x 35 (0.58); the original's box is
  about 57 wide x 56 tall x 59 deep (from Fig. 1, by eye). The AL series' smallest footprint is 64 x 58, so no AL model is closer to the original's 57 mm square; the AL080807 (80 x 75 x 70, 0.88) is more cubic but 40 percent wider than the original. **Position:** camera axis and tilt axis at **z 667**
  (lens centre and the original's motor disc centre both measure z 667 to 668 in the front view; the side view agrees; [S12] states 660 and an earlier side-view reading gave 664: the owner asked for the closest match to the original), camera at y -28 [S8], so the box edge is at y +4 and the disk fills the 15.5 mm from there to the web.
  **Hood:** the window is square to the box (it was turned 8 degrees, from the flight photo [S5] Fig. 8; the CAD front view [S18] shows it square, and the owner asked for it straightened).
- **Hat is a real antenna (decided 2026-11-08, owner chose option 2):** the original's plate is its S-band patch antenna (`original-dimensions.md` 3m), on the gimbal atop the camera box. The replica's hat is a **GNSS (GPS L1) patch** on an **aluminium ground-plane disc**: the disc is Ø70 x 1.5 mm sheet, bonded to the box top (z 696 to 697.5), and a 35 x 35 x 4 mm ceramic patch (passive, of the Taoglas HP5354.A class, retailer listing; mass 15 g is an assumption) sits on it, top at **z 701.5, 1.5 mm over the stated 700**. It feeds a coax that follows the camera ribbon through the disk's cable spool. A GNSS receiver module (10 g allowance, not placed) sits in the body. The patch tilts with the camera, so GPS reception is best with the camera level; with the camera tilted down 90 degrees the patch faces forward. Other ideas not taken: a 2.4 GHz circularly polarized ceramic patch (Taoglas WPC.25A, 25 x 25 x 4 mm, 6 dBi) or a hand-built air-spaced aluminium patch (the paper models 65 mm across over a 0.6 mm gap, 2.42 GHz); ready-made active GNSS patches are too tall (Taoglas ADFGP.60A: Ø80 x 19 mm).
- **2.4 GHz antenna, on the body (decided 2026-11-08):** after the original's sleeve dipole (the rover-to-lander antenna, 2.23 dBi; 55.7 mm long, 159.5 mm with its tube in the paper's model), vertical on the top face of the body **290 mm behind the mast** (x -129, y +58 by my reading of the figure, unverified), `hardware/body/antenna.py`. Off the shelf, at the paper's length: the **AIR802 ANRD2403-RSP** (3 dBi, 13.5 x 158 mm, black, RP-SMA plug; retailer listing) is modelled, on a 6 mm aluminium block, 158 mm tall (top at z 512); the 5 dBi ANRD2405-RPSMA (157.4 mm) is the alternative. The listings do not say whether the plug is straight or angled, nor give a mass (15 g assumed). Shorter ones: Taoglas GW.20.A151W (89.5 mm, white), Adafruit 944 swivel (100 mm, 9.92 g), LSR (105 mm, black). The Pi Zero 2 W has no antenna connector, so a USB WiFi adapter with an RP-SMA connector (10 g allowance) feeds it. This gives the replica a real 2.4 GHz link on an antenna in the original's place.
- **Lens (off the shelf, 2026-11-08):** the original's lens is a 3D Plus CASPEX 3DCM739 camera with a Lambda-X 7.8 mm, f/8, 82 x 82 degree optic [S12], which cannot be bought. The replica uses the **Raspberry Pi High Quality Camera (CS mount, Sony IMX477, 12.3 MP, 7.9 mm diagonal sensor)** and a **4 mm CS-mount lens**:
  the HQ camera is a 38 x 38 mm board (four Ø2.5 holes 30 x 30 apart, a Ø36 focus-ring mount 10.35 mm deep, the board 1.4 mm, connectors 5.7 mm behind; Raspberry Pi product brief, Jan 2023, p. 4; stated), close to the CASPEX's 35 x 35 footprint, and it takes any CS or C lens, so the lens can be changed later (`electronics-spec.md`).
  The 4 mm lens (a generic 5 MP, 1/2", f/1.2, fixed iris, manual focus, 80 degrees horizontal, 30 x 42 mm, 49 g; one retailer's listing, no maker named, so treat the figures as unverified) gives about 76 degrees horizontal on the IMX477 (arithmetic from the sensor width), near the original's 82. Other options: the official 6 mm lens (63 degrees, 30 x 34 mm, 53 g, stated by retailers),
  a 3.2 mm CS lens (113 to 120 degrees), or the Camera Module 3 Wide (102 x 67 degrees, 25 x 24 mm board, 4 g), which is 0.09 kg lighter but has no changeable lens and a board too large to sit behind the baffle ring. The HQ camera (about 50 g by a retailer listing, CS variant assumed) and the lens (49 g) add 0.09 kg over the earlier 10 g stand-in. A Pi Zero 2 W needs a Zero camera cable (the HQ camera's is the 15-pin one).
  In the model the lens front is 8 mm behind the hood face (like the original's recessed front element), the board and mount sit in the box (board front face at x 154), and the lens barrel passes through the Ø35 cover hole and a Ø31.2 bore in the baffle ring; the hood window is 8 mm in front of the glass, so it does not vignette a 76 degree field.
- **Tilt servo mounting (2026-11-08):** the STS3215 (Waveshare ST3215 drawing, 2022: 45.22 x 24.72 x 32 mm body, 37.25 over the horns) has **four holes on each end face, 25.4 mm apart along the long side and 20.5 across**, at 7.4 and 32.8 mm from the shaft along the body (read from the drawing, scale 5.1 px/mm: plus or minus 0.3 mm), and the output
  horn is a Ø19.2 disc with four holes on a Ø14 circle and a centre screw. The thread is not stated (retailers say M3; unverified). The servo stands against the outer (+y) face of the bracket web with its horn through the Ø19.2 pass-through hole, held by **four M3 screws from the web's inner face** into its front-face holes (the web has four Ø3.4 holes at x +-10.25, z 659.6 and 634.2); the screw heads lie in the 0.5 to 2.5 mm gap between the web and the disk, where the disk's annular recess clears them (use low-profile pan heads, not countersunk, which a 2 mm sheet
  cannot take). The servo sits 5.6 mm clear of each fin, and its rear end (y 56.7) is free. Load: the servo's stall torque is 1.91 N.m (`electronics-spec.md`), so the four screws on a roughly 16 mm radius see about 30 N each at stall; the camera unit's own weight moment is about 0.1 N.m. The web's bearing and the screw threads in the servo's case are not checked.
- **Camera link (decided 2026-11-08, owner chose option A; routing revised the same day after the owner's photographs):** the Arducam LAN cable extension kit (CSI-2 over an RJ45 patch cable, rated to 10 m, works with the HQ camera; Pi Zero use not stated by the listings; boards of about 35 x 25 mm are an assumption, their sizes and masses were not found, the package weighs 20 to 50 g). The camera's short ribbon goes through a 20 x 3 mm slot in the box's -y wall into the **Tx board in an aluminium pod on the box's -y wall** (32 x 21 x 34 mm, 1.5 mm walls; where the original has a blue connector housing), the **Rx board hangs under the deck** beside the Pi, and a short ribbon runs from it to the Pi. **The round cable hangs loose** (`headb.camera_link_cable`, a Ø5.5 mm sweep): from the pod's floor it swings out on the -y side and drops to a Ø16 gland hole in the deck at x 112, y -36 (over the Rx board's jack, beside the base plate). **This is how the original does it:** in the photographs of the flight-model rover (supplied by the owner, 2026-11-08) the orange Kapton-wrapped harness from the head and the antenna leaves the head's -y side connector and hangs in free loops beside the mast to the body, not routed through the mast or clipped along it; ours is the same idea, and it lets the cord's 13.5 x 12 mm plug pass anywhere (an earlier version of this design routed the cord down the extrusion slot, through a raised flange and a cable chamber above the lid, which was dropped). Length from the CAD (`hardware/mast/cable_route.py`): 307 mm from the pod's jack to the gland and 13 mm to the Rx jack, plus 144 mm of pan slack (the pod circles the axis at 72 mm), 100 mm of tilt slack and 40 mm of strain relief: 604 mm, 664 mm with a 10 percent allowance, against the kit's 1 m cord. The same loop style carries the GNSS coax and the tilt servo's wires (the coax is a second, separate cable down the -y side). Mass: the pod and Tx board (0.027 kg), the Rx board (0.010) and the cord (0.030): **+0.067 kg** against the plain ribbon; the replica goes over 10 kg. Not checked: the real boards' sizes; a loose loop's behaviour (snagging on the box, fatigue at the pod's jack); that the kit's Tx accepts the 15-pin ribbon without the 15 to 22 pin cables it ships with.
- **Disk, its function (resolved 2026-11-08):** a hollow **turned aluminium** shell (6061 bar, 4 mm side wall, 2 mm end wall, 40 mm across, y 4 to 19.5, 0.021 kg; it was printed at 0.010 kg until the owner asked for metal; made on a lathe and a drill, with the M3 threads tapped straight into the metal and no inserts, the rim slot milled and its edges rounded and deburred for the ribbon) that does two jobs. (1) **Coupling:** it turns with the servo horn and carries the camera box: a Ø19.2 boss at the web end sits on the horn (four screws on the Ø14 pattern and a centre screw), and the open end is bolted to the box's +y wall by four M3 on a Ø36 circle (clearance holes in the 3.7 mm cast wall, M3 threads tapped in the shell's ring; assembly order: disk on the horn first, then the box on the disk).
  (2) **Cable spool:** the camera-link cable arrives along the box's underside, passes through an 18 mm slot in the rim at the bottom (it no longer goes through the box wall), loops round the rim and is clamped to the bracket fin's edge; with a +-90 degree tilt the length changes by about 31 mm each way, so about 70 mm of slack is needed. It carries no sensor (the servo has its own 12-bit magnetic encoder, retailer listing) and no tilt hard stop is designed. This replaces the earlier "encoder or mounting, unknown" disk; in the original the round motor of that size sits there (about 56 mm across and 20.7 mm thick, `original-dimensions.md` 3m), which ours does not copy. Section view: `docs/images/disk-section.png`.
